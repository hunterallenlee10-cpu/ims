## What a Z-score measures

A Z-score tells you how far a value sits from its own average, measured in standard deviations:

**Z = (current value − long-run average) ÷ standard deviation**

A reading of 0 means "about average". A reading of +2 means "two standard deviations above average", which is unusual. A reading of −2 is unusually low.

## Reading the dial

| Z-score | Plain-English read |
| --- | --- |
| Below −2 | Unusually cheap against its own history |
| −2 to −1 | Cheaper than usual |
| −1 to +1 | Around normal |
| +1 to +2 | More expensive than usual |
| Above +2 | Unusually expensive against its own history |

## Where it falls short

- History is not destiny. A market can stay "expensive" for a long time.
- The average itself can drift as conditions change.
- A single number hides detail. That is why valuation is only one of three inputs.
